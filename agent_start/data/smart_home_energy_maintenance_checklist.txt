# Smart Home Energy Management - Maintenance and Seasonal Checklist

## Regular Checks

1. Review the dashboard for unexplained spikes or sustained baseload changes.

2. Check thermostat schedules against current occupancy.

3. Confirm major automations are not conflicting.

4. Review smart-plug schedules for obsolete rules.

5. Check HVAC filter condition as required by equipment and environment.

6. Keep supply and return airflow paths unobstructed.

7. Review hot-water recirculation schedules if present.

8. Check EV charging schedule against current driving needs.

9. Verify solar, battery, and energy-monitor gateways are online.

10. Investigate repeated device disconnects rather than ignoring missing data.

## Monthly Checks

11. Compare daily average energy use with a comparable prior period.

12. Review overnight baseload.

13. Review recurring peak intervals.

14. Document new devices that changed the load profile.

15. Export or back up data when retention is limited.

16. Review account security and remove unused integrations.

17. Check trusted firmware updates.

18. Review automation logs for repeated failures.

19. Inspect accessible weather stripping and obvious air leaks.

20. Check accessible plumbing for hot-water leaks.

## Heating-Season Preparation

21. Service heating equipment when required.

22. Clean or replace HVAC filters as required.

23. Confirm thermostat schedules and heat-pump settings.

24. Clear supply and return vents.

25. Inspect accessible duct connections for damage.

26. Check door and window weather stripping.

27. Inspect attic-hatch sealing where accessible.

28. Review winter humidity and ventilation strategy.

29. Test freeze-protection or minimum-temperature rules.

30. Verify backup-power priorities if winter outages are a concern.

## Cooling-Season Preparation

31. Check cooling-system maintenance needs before peak weather.

32. Clean or replace filters as required.

33. Confirm thermostat cooling schedules.

34. Review shading schedules for hot exposures.

35. Keep outdoor HVAC equipment clear as specified by the manufacturer.

36. Review dehumidifier settings and drainage.

37. Inspect window and door sealing.

38. Review time-of-use schedules before high summer prices when applicable.

39. Verify safe temperature limits for vulnerable occupants.

40. Check that weather-based automations use the correct location.

## Smart Device and Data Maintenance

41. Use unique credentials and multifactor authentication where available.

42. Remove integrations that are no longer used.

43. Keep routers and smart-home hubs updated.

44. Prefer supported devices that still receive security updates.

45. Document which automations depend on cloud services.

46. Test manual control for critical devices.

47. Check battery-powered sensors before relying on occupancy automation.

48. Review geofencing permissions after phone or OS changes.

49. Do not silently fill missing energy intervals with zero.

50. Document meter resets or replacements.

51. Keep unit conversions consistent.

52. Check for duplicate devices after integration changes.

53. Record solar and battery sign conventions.

54. Verify daily totals against interval sums periodically.

55. Keep tariff versions and effective dates with cost calculations.

## Professional Service Triggers

56. Repeated breaker trips.

57. Burning smell, hot outlets, or discolored electrical connections.

58. Gas or combustion concerns.

59. Refrigerant or HVAC safety concerns.

60. Leaking water-heater tank.

61. Persistent HVAC short cycling or failure to maintain safe temperature.

62. Major unexplained continuous loads that cannot be isolated safely.

63. Electrical panel or circuit work requiring qualified service.

64. Structural, moisture, or mold problems found during envelope work.

65. Any energy-saving change that would bypass equipment safety controls.

Notes:
- Recommendations are general educational guidance, not a substitute for an on-site energy audit, licensed HVAC/electrical work, or utility-specific tariff advice.
- Costs, savings, climate effects, and payback periods vary by home, equipment, occupancy, weather, and local utility rates.
