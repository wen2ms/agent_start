# Smart Home Energy Management - Efficiency and Optimization Guide

## Measurement Before Optimization

1. Establish a reliable baseline before claiming savings.

2. Use comparable periods and normalize for billing-period length.

3. Separate whole-home measured energy from device-level estimates.

4. Track weather-sensitive loads separately when possible.

5. Document occupancy changes.

6. Measure major flexible loads such as EV charging separately when possible.

7. Keep solar generation, battery flows, grid import, and export separate.

8. Record automation changes.

9. Mark missing data explicitly.

10. Validate units before combining datasets.

## Heating and Cooling

11. Use schedules that reduce unnecessary conditioning during predictable unoccupied periods.

12. Avoid extreme setpoint changes that create discomfort or interact poorly with controls.

13. Maintain filters and airflow paths according to equipment guidance.

14. Keep thermostat sensors away from direct sun, drafts, and strong local heat sources when possible.

15. Investigate persistent short cycling or unusually long runtime.

16. Seal obvious building-envelope leaks where appropriate.

17. Improve insulation where an assessment identifies deficiencies.

18. Seal and insulate ducts where appropriate.

19. Use shading to reduce unwanted solar heat gain while preserving useful daylight.

20. Treat humidity control as part of comfort and HVAC performance.

## Plug Loads and Appliances

21. Identify devices with meaningful standby consumption before automating shutoff.

22. Use smart plugs for measurement only where ratings and appliance type permit.

23. Group entertainment peripherals on controllable strips when appropriate.

24. Schedule noncritical equipment only when it does not interfere with safety or required operation.

25. Keep refrigerator and freezer door seals in good condition.

26. Maintain appliance ventilation clearance.

27. Use full laundry and dishwasher loads when practical.

28. Use lower-temperature laundry options when appropriate.

29. Air-dry clothes or dishes when practical.

30. Replace equipment based on measured use, condition, total cost, and efficiency - not age alone.

## Lighting

31. Replace frequently used inefficient lamps with efficient LEDs.

32. Use occupancy controls where lights are often left on accidentally.

33. Use daylight when it reduces electric lighting without excessive glare or heat gain.

34. Set sensible occupancy-sensor timeouts.

35. Use compatible dimming where useful.

36. Consolidate overlapping scenes and schedules.

37. Keep manual control available.

38. Audit exterior-lighting schedules seasonally.

## Water Heating

39. Fix hot-water leaks promptly.

40. Reduce unnecessary hot-water use.

41. Use safe water-heater temperature settings.

42. Insulate appropriate hot-water pipes where beneficial.

43. Control recirculation pumps with schedules or demand controls where appropriate.

44. Review long recirculation loops for heat loss.

45. Consider efficient fixtures when they meet user needs.

46. Consider heat-pump water heating where installation conditions are suitable.

## Tariffs and Flexible Loads

47. Understand the utility tariff before optimizing for cost.

48. Shift EV charging when tariff and driving needs make it useful.

49. Stagger flexible high-power loads when demand charges matter.

50. Do not shift loads for insignificant benefit.

51. Use battery schedules that reflect backup, tariff, and lifecycle goals.

52. Keep preheating and precooling within comfort and equipment constraints.

53. Record tariff changes.

54. Analyze cost and energy independently.

## Automation Quality

55. Give each automation a clear purpose.

56. Use explicit triggers and stop conditions.

57. Add hysteresis or delays to noisy thresholds.

58. Define manual override behavior.

59. Define safe behavior when connectivity fails.

60. Prevent conflicting rules.

61. Use occupancy only where sensing is reliable.

62. Test automations in small steps.

63. Log important automation events.

64. Re-evaluate schedules after seasonal or occupancy changes.

## Reporting and AI

65. Separate measured facts from inferred explanations.

66. State data limitations near affected conclusions.

67. Use cautious language for correlations.

68. Do not infer a faulty appliance from whole-home data alone.

69. Do not treat missing values as zero.

70. Avoid unsupported percentage-savings claims.

71. Use daily averages when periods differ.

72. Include weather context for HVAC-heavy comparisons.

73. Rank recommendations by impact, effort, cost, and confidence.

74. Escalate safety-critical issues to qualified professionals.

Notes:
- Recommendations are general educational guidance, not a substitute for an on-site energy audit, licensed HVAC/electrical work, or utility-specific tariff advice.
- Costs, savings, climate effects, and payback periods vary by home, equipment, occupancy, weather, and local utility rates.
