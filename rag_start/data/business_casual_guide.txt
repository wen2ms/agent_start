Business Casual Dress Guide

1. General Principle
Business casual is less formal than traditional business attire but more polished than everyday casual clothing.
Clothing should be clean, well-fitting, and appropriate for a professional environment.

2. Common Men's Business Casual Clothing
Oxford shirts, polo shirts, sweaters, chinos, dress trousers, loafers, and clean leather shoes are common choices.
A blazer can make an outfit more formal without requiring a full suit.
Dark, clean jeans may be acceptable in some workplaces, but company policy should be checked first.

3. Common Women's Business Casual Clothing
Blouses, button-down shirts, sweaters, tailored trousers, midi skirts, simple dresses, loafers, flats, and low heels are common choices.
Blazers and cardigans can add structure to an outfit.
Avoid clothing that is overly revealing or designed primarily for athletic or beach use.

4. Color Choices
Navy, charcoal, black, beige, brown, white, and light blue are safe professional colors.
Subtle patterns such as small checks or thin stripes are generally appropriate.
Bright colors can be used as accents.

5. Interview Clothing
When unsure about a company's dress code, it is usually safer to dress slightly more formally.
A blazer, collared shirt or blouse, tailored trousers, and clean closed-toe shoes are reliable choices.
Avoid large graphics, visibly worn clothing, and excessive accessories.

6. Remote Meetings
For video meetings, prioritize a neat upper-body appearance and avoid distracting patterns.
Solid colors usually appear clearly on camera.
Good lighting and a simple background also contribute to a professional appearance.
