Clothing Color and Styling Guide

1. Everyday Wear
Neutral colors such as black, white, gray, navy, beige, and brown are easy to combine.
For a simple outfit, use one neutral base color and one accent color.
Denim blue works well with white, gray, black, olive, and many earth tones.

2. Business and Professional Settings
Navy, charcoal, black, white, light blue, and beige are common professional colors.
For interviews and formal meetings, avoid overly bright colors, large logos, and distracting patterns.
A navy blazer with a white or light-blue shirt is a common business-casual combination.

3. Casual Settings
Casual outfits can use brighter colors and stronger contrast.
White sneakers, denim, neutral T-shirts, and lightweight jackets are versatile wardrobe basics.
Patterns such as stripes and checks are suitable when the rest of the outfit remains simple.

4. Seasonal Colors
Spring: light blue, soft green, lavender, pale yellow, and other fresh colors.
Summer: white, light gray, sky blue, beige, and other light colors.
Fall: camel, olive, rust, burgundy, brown, and other earth tones.
Winter: black, charcoal, navy, forest green, burgundy, and deep red.

5. Basic Color-Matching Rules
Use no more than two or three dominant colors for a clean outfit.
Neutral colors can connect two stronger colors.
Matching different shades of the same color creates a coordinated monochromatic look.
When wearing a bright statement piece, keep the remaining items more subdued.

6. Accessories
Black and brown leather accessories are widely used with business and casual outfits.
Metallic accessories such as silver and gold can function as neutral accents.
A colorful scarf, bag, tie, or pair of shoes can add contrast without dominating the outfit.
